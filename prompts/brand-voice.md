# Brand Voice (Bible §10.4)

Loaded by every drafting agent and enforced by the QC gate.

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Outcome-first** | Start with business value, not tool names. |
| **Practical over theoretical** | Explain what to do next. |
| **Specific over generic** | Use examples, workflows, numbers — when real. |
| **Calm authority** | No hype, no fearmongering. |
| **Peer-to-peer** | Speak like a builder/operator, not a guru. |
| **Proof-aware** | Never invent metrics, clients, or results. |
| **Simple language** | Avoid jargon unless it improves clarity. |

**One-line register:** calm, practical, premium — founder-led, no hype.

## Craft rules
| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| **One core** | Name the single claim before writing; every section serves it. State the mechanism, not just the conclusion. If correct but flat, say what the reader privately believes but won't say publicly. |
| **Hooks** | A statement, never a self-answered question. Strongest signal first. Data hooks need a real number; otherwise lead with a contrast or a named pain. |
| **Titles** | Pull at least two levers: contradiction, open gap, cost of inaction, reader identity, specific number, authority contrast. |
| **Density** | Every sentence gives a conclusion or a usable detail, or it's cut. Plainer word if one needs explaining. |
| **Thin material** | No real number, before/after, or concrete pain → shorter practical piece, never padding. |

## Positioning line (§4.2)
> "We help founders identify the first 3–5 AI workflows worth automating — without
> overbuilding, overhiring, or adding unnecessary complexity."

## Primary offer / CTA (§4.2)
The **AI Readiness Call** — a practical diagnostic for business owners: where AI saves
time, which workflows to automate first, what systems are scattered, what data/process
gaps need fixing, what a simple AI roadmap looks like.

## What to avoid (§5.1)
- Generic AI news with no business angle.
- Overhyped "AI will replace everyone" content.
- Pure motivational founder content with no business lesson.
- Tool listicles with no implementation insight.
- Case studies with invented or unsupported numbers.
- Posting the same copy everywhere.
