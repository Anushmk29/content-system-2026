# Content Studio: Every Feature, Explained Simply

**What this is:** a list of every feature in the Content Studio. Each one says what it does, how you use it and why we have it, in easy words.

**The big idea:** the Content Studio is a helper robot for social media. It finds ideas, writes posts, makes pictures, asks you "is this OK?", and then posts them for you at the right time. You are the boss: nothing goes out until you say yes.

**Two helpers inside:**
- **Claude** is the writer. It writes posts, scripts and pictures.
- **Jev** is the judge. It decides things, like "is this a good idea?" or "which platform suits this?".

Keeping writing and judging apart makes both of them better.

---

## 1. Getting in

### 1.1 Sign in
You type a password to open the studio. There are two kinds of people: the **client** (the brand owner) and the **admin** (the person who looks after the machine). We have it so only the right people can see and change your content.

### 1.2 Sign out
The **Sign out** button at the bottom of the left menu closes the studio for you. Use it on a shared computer so nobody else can use your account.

### 1.3 Safety guards
The studio blocks someone who guesses passwords too many times in a minute, and it only lets the studio's own pages send changes. Error messages never show secret details, and the robot's timer has its own secret key. We have these so strangers can't break in or spend your money.

### 1.4 First-time setup
The first time you come in, a six-step guide asks about you: your name and logo, your voice, who you talk to, what you sell, your colours and fonts, and who you want to learn from. Each step can be skipped and changed later in Brand kit. It takes about ten minutes, and it teaches the robot to sound like you from day one.

## 2. Moving around

### 2.1 Left menu
The menu on the left lists every page in the order you work: **Your day** (Today, Ideas, Approvals, Design, Calendar, Film & post, Board), **Research** (Decode, Videos, Hooks), **Brand** (Brand kit, Examples) and **Growth** (Analytics). Admins also see **Training, Costs and System**. It's in work order so you always know what comes next.

### 2.2 Number badges
Small blue numbers next to a page show how many things wait for you there, like 6 posts to approve. A red badge on System means something is broken. They help you see your to-do at a glance.

### 2.3 Search and commands (Ctrl + K)
Press **Ctrl + K** or click **Search** at the top to open a search box. Type a page, a post, or a robot step to run. It's the fastest way to jump anywhere without clicking through menus.

### 2.4 Run pipeline button
The black **▶ run pipeline** button at the top runs the robot's whole job once: find ideas, score them, write, check, schedule and collect numbers. The small arrow next to it lets you run **just one step**. Normally the robot runs by itself on a timer; this button is for "do it now".

### 2.5 Working animation
While the robot works on something you clicked, a glowing spinning shape shows it is busy. It turns green when the job is done and orange if it failed. That way you know the click worked and you don't click twice.

### 2.6 Post drawer
Clicking any post opens a panel on the right with everything about that post: its words, picture, preview, schedule, results and history. You can do almost everything for one post right there, so you never lose your place on the page behind it.

## 3. Today (home page)

### 3.1 Needs you
A list of the things waiting on you right now, most important first: posts to approve, pictures to make, scripts to film, things that broke. Each one has one big button (**Review**, **Retry**, **Run it now**). It's your morning list, so you don't have to hunt.

### 3.2 Going out today
Shows the posts that will be published today and at what time, with a **Full calendar** link. You can check today's posts before they go out.

### 3.3 This week
Three numbers for the week: how many posts went out compared with your goal, how many ready posts are waiting (your "runway"), and how many people saw your posts. It tells you whether you're on track without reading a report.

### 3.4 Your tasks
A simple to-do list. Type a task, pick a priority, and click a task to move it along (to do → doing → done). The weekly review robot adds tasks here too, so the robot's advice turns into real actions.

### 3.5 Up next
The next few scheduled posts in time order. A quick look at what's coming.

### 3.6 Pipeline health
A folded section showing how posts flow through each stage, your mix of content, each robot step's health and recent activity. It's there when you want to look under the hood, and hidden when you don't.

### 3.7 Refresh my results
A button that pulls the latest numbers (views, likes) for your own posts. Use it when you want fresh numbers now instead of waiting for the weekly refresh.

## 4. Ideas

### 4.1 Capture a signal
A box where you type an idea seed, like a question a customer asked or a trend you saw. You can say where it came from and pick a theme. Your best ideas often come from real life, and this keeps them from getting lost.

### 4.2 Start from…
When adding a signal you can start from something the studio already knows: a winning competitor post, a hook that worked, or one of your themes. It fills in the box for you so you type less.

### 4.3 Automatic signals (market listening)
The robot reads news feeds, Reddit, X (Twitter) and chosen websites on its own and drops new signals in your inbox. It only uses public, allowed sources. You get fresh ideas every day without searching.

### 4.4 Signals waiting
Your inbox of idea seeds. **Make posts** turns one straight into a topic ready to draft, **Drop** throws it away, and **Turn them all into topics** does every one at once. You decide which seeds are worth growing.

### 4.5 Research (turning signals into topics)
The robot reads each signal and writes a clear topic: a title, what customers are saying, and which theme and stage of the buying journey it fits. A messy seed becomes a clean idea you can judge.

### 4.6 Topic score
Jev gives each topic a score out of 25 using five checks: will it bring buyers, will many people see it, does it fit your brand, can we prove it, and can it be reused. Topics get a band: **High – draft now**, **Queue**, **Only if timely** or **Drop**. The best ideas rise to the top so you don't waste posts on weak ones.

### 4.7 Topics to decide
Scored topics, best first, with the score breakdown. Click **Draft this idea** to keep one or **Drop** to remove it. You stay in charge of what gets written.

### 4.8 Angles ("They think… / Actually…")
**See angle options** gives five angles for a topic. Each flips a wrong belief your customers hold into the truth. Pick one with **Use this**, or the strongest is used. A clear angle makes a post feel sharp instead of boring.

### 4.9 Platform plan (pick where to post)
Every idea card shows a plan: which platforms, what kind of post (text, carousel, image, video script) and which writing structure. Jev suggests it. Click **Change** to tick or untick platforms, or **Use Jev's plan** to go back. Platforms connected to your posting tool are picked first; others show "not connected", and you can still tick them to get a draft. You only get posts for the places you want.

### 4.10 Writing structure
Under **Change**, each platform has a writing recipe, like "hook, story, three lessons, question" for LinkedIn. Jev picks one; you can choose another. Each platform likes posts built differently, and this makes posts feel native.

### 4.11 Video and Reddit scripts
On the platform plan you can also tick a script you film yourself: an Instagram or Facebook Reel, a LinkedIn video or a YouTube Short. Reddit posts are written for you to post by hand. They show up on **Film & post**. Video works great, but only you can be on camera.

### 4.12 Draft them now
**Selected · ready to draft** lists the topics you kept. **Draft them now** writes every post in their plans in about a minute and sends them for checking. It writes all platforms in one go so you don't wait.

### 4.13 Auto-pick topics (optional)
An admin setting can let the robot pick the top few topics by itself each run. It's for busy weeks when you trust the scores.

## 5. Writing and checking (happens by itself)

### 5.1 Draft writer
Claude writes each post in your voice for your audience, using your Brand kit, the chosen angle, the platform's recipe and openings that worked before. Every post for one idea is written together, so they match but each fits its platform.

### 5.2 Brand voice everywhere
Every writing and judging job gets the same Brand kit summary: your voice, values, audience, themes, offers and learned rules. Everything sounds like the same person: you.

### 5.3 Human-sounding check
Each draft gets a "how human does it sound" score from 0 to 100. The check looks for robot words, same-length sentences, hedging and too many dashes. If the score is under 70, the robot rewrites it once. People trust posts that sound like a real person.

### 5.4 Quality check (QC gate)
Before you see a post, the robot checks it for quality and risk. Any number or claim without proof gets flagged, and the post is put in a lane: low risk, medium or high. Nothing with a made-up fact can ever be posted. That keeps your brand honest.

### 5.5 Proof for claims
If a post says something like "40% faster", it needs a proof link. Posts with unproven claims are marked **claims without a proof link** and are blocked from posting until fixed, so you never publish something you can't back up.

### 5.6 Decision memory
When the robot already judged the exact same text, it reuses the answer instead of paying again. That saves money and time.

## 6. Approvals

### 6.1 The queue
Every post waiting for your OK, oldest first, with badges for risk, unproven claims and "needs a picture". Click **Review →** to open it. You are the final say before anything goes public.

### 6.2 Step 1: fix the words (optional)
In the post drawer you can edit the hook (first line), the body and the ending, then save. Small fixes are faster to make yourself than to explain.

### 6.3 Stronger first line
A button gives you several new first lines in the style of posts that did well. Click one to swap it in. The first line decides whether people stop scrolling.

### 6.4 Step 2: decide
Three choices:
- **Approve** locks the post and sends it on to be scheduled.
- **Request changes** sends it back with your notes.
- **Reject** throws it away.

Your yes or no is the safety lock on everything.

### 6.5 Change reasons as chips
When you ask for changes, tap a reason ("Too long", "Off-voice", "Weak hook", "Wrong facts", "More specific") and add a note only if you want. Less typing, and the robot understands you better.

### 6.6 Auto-rewrite on change request
When this is switched on, your change request makes the robot rewrite just that one post using your notes, and it is checked again. You don't have to rewrite it yourself.

### 6.7 Learning from your feedback
After a few notes, the robot turns your repeated feedback into up to ten standing rules, like "never open with a question". These rules go into every future post. You can see and delete them in Brand kit. You stop repeating yourself.

### 6.8 Send back for review
An approved post that hasn't gone out yet can be pulled back with **↩ Send back for review**. That's useful when news changes or you spot a mistake late.

### 6.9 Platform copies
At the top of the drawer you see every platform version of the same idea as chips, and you can click to jump between them. You can check that the whole set tells one story.

### 6.10 Preview
Shows how the post will look on that platform, with your name and picture. You see it like your followers will.

### 6.11 History
A list of every decision on the post: who approved, who asked for changes, and when. You always know what happened and why.

## 7. Design (pictures and carousels)

### 7.1 Needs a visual
Every carousel and image post that has no picture yet, shown as a card with its first slide's text. It's your picture to-do list.

### 7.2 Make creative
Pick one of your **Examples** (a design you like) and click **Make creative**. The robot recreates that design with this post's words, your colours, logo, fonts and face, for about 4¢ an image. You get on-brand pictures without a designer.

### 7.3 Make all
One button makes pictures for every post that needs one, using the same example, about a minute each. It saves you clicking card by card.

### 7.4 Use my own image or video
Upload your own picture or video for a post instead. Sometimes a real photo is best.

### 7.5 Slide text
Each card can open **All slides' text** to read every slide before making the picture. That lets you check the words first, which is cheaper than fixing a picture later.

### 7.6 Made · waiting for approval
Posts with a picture, plus a **Next: approve** bar and a **Review and approve →** button. The words and the picture are approved together, so nothing goes out half-ready.

### 7.7 Approved · locked
Approved posts with their picture locked. It shows they are done and safe.

## 8. Calendar and posting

### 8.1 Calendar
A month and week view of planned, scheduled and published posts. Drag an approved post onto a day to set its time. You see your whole posting plan in one picture.

### 8.2 Posting time
In the post drawer, pick a date and time for the post to go out. Without a pick, the robot uses a default hour. You choose the best moment for your audience.

### 8.3 Sending to Postiz
**Postiz** is the tool that actually posts to Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and X. When live sync is on, setting or moving a time sends the post to Postiz right away; otherwise the next robot run does it. At that time Postiz posts by itself, with no click needed.

### 8.4 Only connected platforms
You can only schedule posts on platforms connected in Postiz. Others show "No channel connected". It stops posts silently going nowhere.

### 8.5 Choose channels per post
**Post on (untick to opt out)** shows every connected account for that platform, like your LinkedIn profile and your LinkedIn page. Untick the ones you don't want for this one post; at least one stays ticked. You control exactly where each post goes.

### 8.6 Moving and unplanning
Moving a post on the calendar updates it in Postiz, and removing its time removes it from Postiz. The two always agree.

### 8.7 Tracked links (UTM)
Links in your posts get a tag that says where the click came from. You can see which posts bring visitors to your website.

## 9. Film & post (scripts)

### 9.1 How it works
A short guide at the top: approve the script → film it → click **✓ Filmed** → post it yourself → paste the link. Videos and Reddit posts are never posted by the robot. You always know the next step.

### 9.2 In review
New scripts wait here first. When the check is done, **Review and approve →** appears; before that, the card says to click **run pipeline**. Scripts are checked like any post before you film.

### 9.3 Script card
Each script shows the first line you say (🎙), numbered beats with **[ON SCREEN]** text, **[B-ROLL]** footage ideas and your spoken lines, and the closing line. It's a ready shooting plan, so filming is quick.

### 9.4 ✓ Filmed / Back to film
Mark a script filmed when you've recorded it, or move it back if you need to reshoot. It keeps track of what's done.

### 9.5 Paste the link
After posting, paste the post's link. The robot then tracks its views like any other post, so hand-posted content still counts in your results.

## 11. Decode (learn from competitors)

### 11.1 Competitors
Add the accounts you want to learn from, by name and handle per platform, or remove them. Learning from what already works for others is the fastest way to grow.

### 11.2 Run a Decode
The robot collects your competitors' public posts and scores each against that competitor's own usual numbers, for example "4.2× their usual". **⏸ stop** pauses it. You find their real hits, not just their popular account.

### 11.3 Top posts
Their best posts by how much they beat their usual. You see what works right now in your market.

### 11.4 Performance map
A picture where every dot is a post: further right means more reactions, higher means more comments and shares. Click a dot to look at that post. The winners jump out instantly.

### 11.5 Post anatomy
For a chosen post: its hook, skeleton (structure), format and design, plus the raw caption. You learn *why* a post worked, not just *that* it did.

### 11.6 Which formats win?
Compares formats (carousel, video, text…) by results, for you and for competitors. It shows **What they do that we don't** as gaps. You know which formats to try next.

### 11.7 Hunt score
Each competitor post gets a "worth copying" score that mixes how well it did with how well it fits your brand. Jev answers the fit questions. You copy the right posts, not every viral one.

### 11.8 Paid-reach filter
Posts that look like bought reach (low engagement for their views, or sponsored) are flagged and kept out of hooks and copying. Fake winners don't fool you.

### 11.9 Write my version / Recreate
**Write it for** a platform makes your own post with the same structure and hook type, but your topic, voice and offer. Their numbers, names and claims are never copied. You can also copy their design from the post or your own added images. It takes about a minute, and you get a proven shape with your own story.

### 11.10 Your own results
Once a week the robot also reads your own accounts the same way. Your median becomes "your usual". Then every post can say "2× your usual", so you measure against yourself, which is fair.

## 12. Videos

### 12.1 Video list
Every video Decode found, grouped by the next step (transcribe, analyse, use), with a filter by creator and step. You work through videos in order.

### 12.2 Transcribe
Click **Transcribe** and the robot writes down what the video says, on your own computer. **Re-transcribe** or **Retry** if needed. Words are what make a video easy to study.

### 12.3 Why did it work?
Click **Why did it work?** and Claude explains the video: why it worked, how it's built (hook, beats, ending), what keeps people watching, and what to avoid. Jev checks whether it's safe and on-brand to adapt. You learn the recipe behind a hit video.

### 12.4 Two ideas from each video
Each analysed video gives two ready ideas for your brand that reuse the winning hook and structure. **Write it →** turns one into posts, checked and ready. It's the shortest path from "their hit" to "your post".

## 13. Hooks

### 13.1 Hook library
All the opening lines from competitor and own posts, grouped by pattern type, with filters for platform, format and whose posts. Click to use one. Good openings are reusable tricks.

### 13.2 What works for you
Each hook pattern gets a **learned weight** from your own real results. Patterns that worked for you get used more in new drafts. The robot gets better from your numbers, not guesses.

## 14. Brand kit

### 14.1 Identity
Your brand or personal name and one line saying what you do. Every post is written as you.

### 14.2 Voice guide
Paste 3–10 of your posts, or let the robot fetch your best posts from the last year, and it writes a voice guide. You can read and edit it. Posts then sound like you, not like a robot.

### 14.3 Values
Up to eight things you stand for. Pick from suggestions or add your own. Every draft, script and decision is checked against them, so you never post something that goes against what you believe.

### 14.4 Audience and wrong beliefs
Who you write for, and what they wrongly believe. Each post's angle flips one of these beliefs. Posts hit the real pain of real people.

### 14.5 Offers
What you sell: name, link and call to action. Posts end with the right offer, so content leads to sales.

### 14.6 Themes (pillars)
The topics you post about and what share of posts each gets. Your feed stays balanced instead of all one thing.

### 14.7 Look: logo, colours, fonts, photos
Upload your logo and pick its corner, set brand colours and fonts, and add photos of yourself. Pictures use these automatically. Every picture looks like your brand.

### 14.8 Your platforms and own accounts
Which platforms you use and your own account links (LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube). The robot uses them for your own results. It knows where you live online.

### 14.9 Learned rules
The rules learned from your feedback (see 6.7), each with **Forget** to remove it. You can see what the robot learned and correct it.

### 14.10 Live preview
A sample post updates as you save, so you see the effect of each change immediately.

## 15. Examples (design library)

### 15.1 Add an example
Upload a post design you like (one image, or every page of a carousel in order), give it a name and platform. It's kept for every future post. Your taste becomes reusable.

### 15.2 Make one from an example
Give an example a topic and the robot writes the post and recreates the design with your words, colours, logo and face, for about 4¢ an image. It's a quick way to make a ready post from a look you love.

### 15.3 Manage examples
Rename, delete, view full size, or **Open it on Design**. You also see **What the AI copies** from each one. It keeps the library clean.

### 15.4 Starter examples
A few ready designs come built in so you can start before adding your own. You're never stuck with an empty library.

## 16. Analytics

### 16.1 Results per post
Every published post with its reach, engagement, "vs your usual" and a **next move** tip. Pick a time range. You see what worked and what to do about it.

### 16.2 Best posts
Lists of your best posts by reach, by engagement, and by how much they beat your usual. So you can repeat your winners.

### 16.3 By platform, theme and format
Results grouped by platform, by theme and by format, next to competitors' formats. You put more effort where it pays.

### 16.4 Results missing / Add results
Posts with no numbers yet are listed with **Add results**, where you can type numbers from the platform yourself. Each save is a new snapshot. No post is left uncounted.

### 16.5 Automatic numbers
The robot pulls live numbers for recent posts from Postiz and sorts each post into a box like "high reach, low engagement". It marks each post's results as captured. You get numbers without copying them by hand.

### 16.6 Weekly review
Once a week the robot looks at results and theme balance and writes concrete next-week advice. Each piece of advice becomes a task on Today. Learning turns into action every week.

## 17. Admin pages

### 17.1 Training (teach Jev)
Shows the hard cases where Jev and Claude disagreed. You pick the right answer, and your answers count double. **Download dataset (JSONL)** exports all decisions for training. Jev gets smarter from your judgement.

### 17.2 Jev modes
Jev can watch silently (shadow: it answers, but Claude's answer is used) or lead (primary: Jev's answer is used and Claude only steps in when Jev is unsure). Lead mode can be turned on one task at a time, after Jev agrees with Claude often enough. That keeps changes safe and step by step.

### 17.3 Costs
Every paid call (AI writing, pictures, scraping, Jev), split by feature, by model and per day. It shows the cost per draft, per published post and per Decode run, plus a **CSV ↓** download. You always know where the money goes.

### 17.4 Check against bills
Compares what we logged with what the providers actually billed. You can catch hidden costs or mistakes.

### 17.5 System
The health of every robot step, the time each last ran, a **Run** button for each, the run log, activity, and the actions clients took. When something breaks, you see what and where.

### 17.6 Health check
A small address that says whether the studio is healthy. Clients only see OK or not; admins see details. Monitoring tools can watch it, and secrets stay hidden.

## 18. Behind the scenes

### 18.1 The robot's daily path
In order: market signals → research → topic score → draft → quality check → your approval → schedule in Postiz → your own results → analytics → weekly review. It runs on a timer, or when you click **run pipeline**. Each step is fenced off from the others, so one broken step never stops the rest.

### 18.2 Failures are never silent
If a step or one post fails, it's logged, shown on System and Today with a **Retry** button, and the rest keeps going. You always know when something needs a click.

### 18.3 Cost ledger
Every paid call is written down with what it was for. Costs page and bill checks read from it. No surprise bills.

### 18.4 Saving money
The robot works in small batches, reuses answers it already knows, and caches your long brand summary so it isn't paid for again on every call. Same results for less money.
